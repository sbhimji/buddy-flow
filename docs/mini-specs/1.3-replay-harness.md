# Mini-spec 1.3 — Replay harness

Status: **closed 2026-08-13** (same day). Story: DEV-PLAN Phase 1 → 1.3.

Results: (1) the 2026-08-13 capture (1.0GB, 3 runs incl. a kill-drill hole)
replays identically at `-speed 0` and `-speed 100` — pacing provably content-
neutral; (2) real-time spot check: a dense 2,000-frame slice with a 2.96s
recorded span replayed in 2.959s wall (absolute-schedule pacing; the naive
per-delta version drifted 19% and was replaced before close).

## What

Speed control for capture replay. The 1.2 replay path already drives the
production decoder deterministically at full speed; 1.3 adds pacing so a
recorded session can play back:

- **instant** (`-speed 0`, default) — as fast as disk+CPU allow; acceptance
  runs and batch verification.
- **accelerated** (`-speed N`) — recorded inter-frame gaps divided by N; a
  morning in minutes, for watching metrics evolve.
- **real-time** (`-speed 1`) — frames delivered at recorded wall-clock
  spacing (by capture `recv_ns` deltas); the developer view rehearses a
  Saturday as if it were 09:30 Tuesday.

Pacing changes only delivery timing, never order or content — downstream
state (counts, NBBO, future buckets) must be bit-identical at every speed.
Single sleeps are capped at 30s (with a log line): captures legitimately
contain long dead gaps (a kill-drill hole, the lunch lull replayed at 1×)
that would otherwise stall a replay silently.

## Deliberate scope cuts

- Pacing keys on capture `recv_ns` (arrival spacing), not SIP timestamps —
  replay reproduces what ingest experienced. Event-time simulation is not a
  1.3 concern.
- The DEV-PLAN 1.3 bullet about extracting a shared flat-file reader
  (2026-08-13 review) is **deferred to `docs/backlog.md`**: the CSV path has
  no second consumer yet, both copies are test-guarded, and the Monday-live
  push (2026-08-13) prioritizes the bucket store. Recorded there, not
  dropped.

## Done when

1. The 2026-08-13 capture replayed at `-speed 0` and a high acceleration
   (e.g. `-speed 100`) produces identical counts and identical final NBBO —
   pacing provably does not touch content.
2. A real-time spot check: a slice replayed at `-speed 1` takes wall time ≈
   its recorded span (within a tolerance dominated by the sleep cap).
3. Standing consequence (the story's real point): every subsequent story's
   acceptance criteria run against replayed captures, not live market data.
